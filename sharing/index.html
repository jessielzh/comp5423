COMP5423 Natural Language Processing · PolyU · Fall 2026 · an experiment

A large class,
made interactive with AI.

AI is a paradigm shift, for what we teach and for how we teach it. So this term COMP5423 tried something bold: the whole course was redesigned from scratch with an AI agent. It is an experiment, and still a rough one.

In a small class, the teacher asks, a student answers, and the teacher explains. That exchange is how people learn, and it does not scale to a hundred students. In COMP5423 it does. This is one course's try at what PolyU's Education 4.0 calls interactive, flexible, and personalised learning.

Interactive

How do you make a 3-hour class of 100 students interactive?

One class, many exchanges.

The lecturer and the students take turns. The lecturer teaches and explains; the students feed back what they understood. While they practice, each student also trades answers and explanations with the AI workbook, about 50 times on a class day.

Step 1 of 4 · LectureTeaching with web pages instead of PowerPoint.

Flexible

Interactive web pages, not static PowerPoint.

Press N and the speaker notes slide up, for the lecturer in class and for students reading later. Press O for every slide at once. Click a figure and it fills the screen, so the back row can read it. The demo code and data download straight from the slide. The lecturer writes the content in plain text; the agent writes the HTML.

Flexible

Teaching materials open anywhere, anytime.

Every slide and every question is a web page. It fits any screen and opens at any hour, with nothing to install. Students practice on the bus and reread a lecture on a tablet.

Step 2 of 4 · PracticeLearning by doing.

Personalised

Each student practices at their own pace.

The workbook is an AI-made question bank, and every answer comes with an explanation. Each answer shows at once whether it was right, and why. A missed question waits in Pending until the student gets it right. A passed one can be sent back to practice again, or flagged for later.

Step 3 of 4 · ReviewReinforce the teaching with the hard questions.

Data-driven

The lecturer sees what the room missed, while it practices.

The class report refreshes every 30 seconds. It shows who is practicing and ranks every question by how often it was answered wrong. One click shows how the class split across the options. When practice ends, the lecturer already knows what to review.

Step 4 of 4 · QuizReinforce the learning with a closed-book quiz.

Data-driven

Students who finish the workbook score higher on the quiz.

Every quiz question is drawn from the workbook, and since Quiz 3 from the ones the class missed most. On three of the four quizzes, students who had worked through all the practice before the quiz scored clearly higher than those who had not.

Hands-on

From passive learners to active creators.

The students do not just learn about LLMs. They build with them, on problems they can see and touch.

Every student trains an LLM from scratch, and it writes its own Tang poems.

熟读唐诗三百首，不会作诗也会吟: read three hundred Tang poems well, and you can chant a poem even if you cannot write one. That is also how a language model learns. In Project 1, every student builds a small GPT from scratch and trains it on 24,000 Tang poems. It sounds intimidating, but with a well-designed project it is well within reach, and now every student has a Tang poet of their own.

Every student builds an AI loan officer.

Project 2 is a practical problem. At a made-up Hong Kong bank, a customer describes themselves in a few lines, in English, Chinese or both. The system must read it, find the bank's rules, do the maths, ignore tricks hidden in the text, and answer: approve or reject, and what is the monthly payment? Each step is a topic of the course, and the only model allowed is a small one that runs on a laptop.

Fun

Make it fun.

Hard material does not have to feel hard.

Every student carries the name of a great researcher.

The workbook and the project leaderboards never show a real name. Each student is known instead by a researcher who shaped the field, with a line on why it matters: hover over a name to read it. A small push for students to see themselves in that line. The agent chose all 206 names.

AI-empowered

The agent does the making; the lecturer brings the creativity.

Quick, personal feedback has always worked. What stopped it in a big class was the work behind it. Now the agent does the making: the questions, the website, the slides. That leaves the lecturer the creative part: deciding what is worth teaching, and inventing new ways to teach it. Teaching itself becomes creative, and fun.
